What am I hearing?

The network is a rate model. Each neuron sums its synaptic input through the measured connectome, with excitatory or inhibitory sign from its cell class and weight from its synapse count, then responds through a rectifying, saturating nonlinearity with spike-rate adaptation. Inhibitory cells are faster than pyramidal cells.

The image enters only through the neurons that were imaged in the living mouse. Each is driven by oriented contrast energy at its receptive-field centre and preferred orientation, from the MICrONS digital twin (Wang et al. 2025; Ding et al. 2025), mixed with untuned contrast according to how selective it is. Everything else, including every inhibitory cell and most of L5 and L6, is driven only through the wiring.

Each cell class is a string family; each 30° of preferred orientation is a string. When a string's mean activity rises past threshold, relative to its own response range, it plucks a note: pitch from orientation, octave and instrument from class, stereo position from area. Cells that were not imaged are assigned the orientation and receptive field they acquire in the model, mapped in silico with gratings at 12 angles and flickering patches on a grid; for the imaged cells this mapping recovers their measured preference to within a couple of degrees. Cells that end up broadly tuned play the root, an octave down.

What this is not. The synaptic gains, time constants and nonlinearity were set by hand for stable, musical dynamics, not fit to the recordings, unlike the trained fly model behind FlyHarp. Most axons in the volume are not proofread, so many connections are missing or fragmentary, and the volume has no thalamic cell bodies, so the real feedforward input is stood in for by the measured tuning. Treat it as the MICrONS wiring played through a simple model, not as a simulation of mouse V1.
